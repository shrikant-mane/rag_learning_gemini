Bag of Words (BoW) is a technique that converts text into numerical vectors based on 
how frequently words appear in a document.

BoW ignores the order of words and focuses on their occurrences.

doc1 = "python is easy python"
doc2 = "java is easy"

vocabulary = ["python", "is", "easy", "java"]

hen count the occurrences of each word.
Document	python	is	easy	java
doc1	    2	    1	1	    0
doc2	    0	    1	1	    1

Notice that "python" has a value of 2 because it appears twice in Document 1.


Feature	            One-Hot Encoding	    Bag of Words
Represents	        Individual word	        Entire document
Vector values	    0 or 1	                Word counts
Word frequency	    No	                    Yes
Word order	        Not represented	        Ignored
Sparse representation	Yes	                Usually yes


limitations

    - No semantic understanding: "car" and "automobile" are different words.
    - No word order: "dog bites man" and "man bites dog" produce identical vectors.
    - Large vocabulary: Thousands of unique words can create very high-dimensional vectors.
    - No importance weighting: Common words such as "the" and "is" may contribute to scores even though they are less informative.
    - Frequency bias: Documents repeating query words can receive high scores.
    
These limitations motivate techniques such as TF-IDF and BM25.
